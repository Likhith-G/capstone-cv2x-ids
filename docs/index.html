What received power can and cannot tell you about a lying vehicle

Cars broadcast their position several times a second, and every message is cryptographically signed. The signature proves who sent it. It proves nothing about whether it is true. This is a labelled dataset of vehicles lying to each other over C-V2X, and a study of what a receiver can actually catch.

The short version

A single receiver is blind to a position lie, and many receivers are not Four learner families reach 0.010, 0.052 and 0.167 on the three constant-offset classes, because one receiver at a fixed geometry supplies one equation for four unknowns. Pooling received power across receivers recovers detection down to a floor at 47.2 m. The array's geometry then predicts the direction an adaptive attacker will lie in, 79.3 degrees off the road axis against 75 to 85 measured by independent search. That prediction holds while the estimator's error stays roughly elliptical, which was measured by sweeping the propagation law until it stopped being so.
