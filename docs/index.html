Catching connected cars that lie about where they are

Cars broadcast their position several times a second, and every message is cryptographically signed. The signature proves who sent a message. It cannot prove that the position inside is true. This project measures what receivers can catch, using only what a message claimed and what their radios measured, over the 5G sidelink cars use to talk directly.

The short version

One receiver is blind to a consistent position lie, and many receivers are not A car that shifts every claim by the same amount passes every content check. Five learner families at one receiver reach at most 0.158 on it. Receivers that pool their measured power before deciding detect it above a floor of 47.3 m, with a 95 percent interval of 40.6 to 57.3 m; receivers that share only their verdicts detect nothing. The receivers' geometry predicts the direction an informed attacker will lie, 79.7 degrees off the road against 75 to 85 found by search, and a map check covers that direction. The detector can be learned across roadside units without sharing raw data Trained by federated learning across roadside unit regions and scored on cars no region had heard, it comes within about 5 percent of central training on validation and is level with it on test. Secure aggregation costs no detection. When traffic gets denser or transmit power drops, refitting the detector's input scaling on unlabelled local data recovers 77 and 87 percent of the loss.
