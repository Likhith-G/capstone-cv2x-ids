Two ways to listen

One minute of simulated highway traffic. Some cars are lying about where they are, and every lie is correctly signed. The same receivers judge every car twice. On top, each receiver decides alone and they vote. Below, they share what their radios measured and decide once, together. Both are set to raise the same number of false alarms on honest cars, so the comparison is fair.

Positions are the simulator's transmit log for one seed of the reference scenario. Each verdict comes from a detector that never saw that car during training: both are random forests trained with folds grouped by physical transmitter, the protocol of the report. "Deciding alone" counts every receiver's own verdict and flags a car once enough of them say it is lying; that share is set, on honest cars alone, as low as it can go without raising more false alarms over the minute than sharing does, because a plain majority vote flags almost nothing and would make the comparison unfair. "Sharing" is the pooled detector over the same receivers' measurements. Generated by analysis/make_booth_replay.py. A second with fewer than five receivers, or one where the car crosses the end of the simulated road, has no shared decision and is drawn grey. The across-road scale is exaggerated so the lanes can be seen.
