Four moves

In order. Each one only makes sense after the one before it.

  1. Lie with one receiver listeningGo as far as you like. Nothing happens. One receiver does not do this badly, it cannot do it at all.
  2. Switch the other receivers onNow there is a boundary. The panel keeps your smallest successful lie, so this is a game: get it as low as you can. Find the smallest lie you still get away with and you have found the detection floor yourself. Watch the flag threshold fall as you add receivers: it is 4.95 with five and 1.21 with all of them, because honest cars stop looking suspicious too.
  3. Lie sideways instead of along the roadYou get away with much more. The receivers sit in a line, so across the road they can barely see. Distance is not the whole story: a hundred metres sideways can be safer than fifty along.
  4. Turn on the map constraintThe detector now knows a car cannot be off the carriageway. The sideways trick stops working.
Every number on this page was computed by analysis/make_booth_surface.py over the real dataset, using the same consistency statistic the paper reports. The page is a lookup over that measurement sampled on a grid, not an illustration of it. The threshold is the 95th percentile of the ratio over honest stations, so caught means the claim looks worse than 95 in 100 honest cars. That threshold is per receiver count, which is why it moves: with five receivers the fit has four unknowns and almost no slack, so honest cars scatter and nothing separates. Adding receivers tightens the honest distribution before it improves detection, and the second depends on the first.
