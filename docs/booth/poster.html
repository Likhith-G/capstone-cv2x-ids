The problem

Connected cars broadcast their position several times a second over a direct radio link, and the cars around them brake and merge on it. Every message is signed, which proves which car sent it. The signature says nothing about whether the position is true, so a car with valid credentials can sign a false position and every cryptographic check still passes.

A receiver has one independent piece of evidence: how strongly the signal arrived, which depends on where the sender really is.

Our four aims

  1. One receiver. Can a single receiver tell that a correctly signed position is false?
  2. Many receivers. Can receivers that pool their measurements tell, and from what size of lie?
  3. A smart attacker. Where would an informed attacker lie, and what stops it?
  4. Deployment. Can roadside units learn the detector together without sharing raw data, and does it keep working when conditions change?

What we built

We simulated a highway in the ns-3 network simulator with the 5G sidelink module, on which cars talk to each other directly [2, 3]. Honest cars carry realistic positioning error; attackers carry out ten kinds of misbehaviour, from false positions to flooding the channel. Every receiver records what each message claimed and what its radio measured. We found no public misbehaviour dataset over a 5G sidelink that records this.

Conclusions against the aims

  1. One receiver cannot detect a steady position lie at any size: content checks score 0.000 and the best of five learning methods 0.158 (Figure 3). The public VeReMi datasets show the same [4].
  2. Pooled receivers detect it above 47.3 m, or 55.3 m when each roadside unit pools only its own area (Figure 3).
  3. The receivers' geometry predicts where an informed attacker lies, and a map check covers that direction (Figure 4).
  4. Roadside units can learn the detector together (Figure 5). After denser traffic or a weaker radio, refitting its input scaling on unlabelled local data recovers 77 and 87 percent of the loss; attackers lying in bursts cost only 0.0230 and refitting does not help.

Limitations and next steps

Every result comes from simulating one straight highway, and the radio model has not been checked against real measurements. The next step is to fit the same propagation model to real sidelink measurements, then test a junction and a curve, where the receivers no longer sit in a line.
