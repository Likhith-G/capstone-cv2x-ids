C-V2X sidelink · misbehaviour detection

A car can lie about where it is. The radio can catch it.

Connected vehicles broadcast their position several times a second, signed under a valid certificate. The signature proves who sent the message. It proves nothing about whether the position inside is true. We measure exactly how big a lie has to be before anyone can tell.

Why the honest cars matter

Benign vehicles here do not report their exact position. They carry a realistic receiver error, median four metres.

Without it the honest class has no positional spread, every lie is separable in principle, and the detector is asked a far easier question than deployment would ever ask it. Most published results are measured that way.

What we do not claim

That small lies are detectable. They are not, and the floor is the finding rather than a limitation to apologise for.

That the radio catches radio-layer attacks. In this simulator it cannot; the cross-layer result is radio features catching application-layer misbehaviour.

Generated in ns-3.42 with 5G-LENA over an NR V2X Mode 2 PC5 sidelink, eight seeds on a 6 km carriageway. Ground truth never crosses the air interface: the transmitter logs what is true, each receiver logs only what it received, and the two are joined offline. Ten adversarial integrity gates run before any model is trained, and a nearest-neighbour classifier reaches only 0.35 on this data, which is the evidence the task is not being won by memorisation.
